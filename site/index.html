Execution model & reproducibility

  1. Original EMA bias: previous completed daily close versus that same day's EMA20. EMA uses α = 2/21, seeded at the first daily close on 1 Jan 2015, with over a year of warm-up. Current-day daily data never influences today's trade.
  2. Original EMA filter: |previous close / previous EMA20 − 1| ≤ selected percentage. Equal close and EMA means no directional bias.
  3. Range: high and low of every bar from ORB start through ORB end, inclusive. Default 09:20–09:59 contains 40 bars.
  4. Breakout: original strategy uses the EMA bias and ORB close breakout. The PDH/PDL variant ignores EMA entirely: first eligible close strictly above both ORB high and previous-day high is LONG; strictly below both ORB low and previous-day low is SHORT. Wick-only crossings do not qualify. Fill is the breakout bar's close; a gap outside the range qualifies. Maximum one trade per day.
  1. Risk: stop distance = selected multiplier × range. Target = selected R × initial risk. Levels use exact arithmetic without tick rounding.
  2. Exit: stop/target become active on the next candle after entry. A stop gap fills at the worse open; a favorable target gap fills only at the target. If both levels occur within a bar and neither is resolved by the open, stop wins. Levels have priority over force exit on 15:10.
  3. Metrics: R = signed index points / initial stop distance. Cost-adjusted R subtracts round-trip points / initial risk. R profit factor is gross positive R / absolute negative R. Points profit factor uses point P&L. Fixed quantity = lot size × number of lots, default 65 × 1. ₹ P&L and ₹ risk = respective point amount × quantity; cost in ₹ = selected round-trip point cost × quantity. No compounding, margin or account-return claim. The user-selected size is applied uniformly across all dates, not as a historical contract specification.
  4. Limitations: historical OHLC does not establish tick order, executable liquidity or achievable close fills. Index prices and the trading instrument may differ. This is one historical sample, with no holdout validation or parameter optimization. Repeated input experiments introduce selection bias.
